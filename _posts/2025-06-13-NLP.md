# Introduction

This project was carried out as part of CSI 500 at George Mason University Korea, under the guidance of Professor Sohyun Park. I found the concept of handling text data numerically and extracting meaningful information from it to be both interesting and practical. In particular, in this project, I learned about **Topic Modeling** techniques for finding hidden themes in text based on **Movie/TV review data** from Amazon, and gained insight into how such data can be utilized in real-world applications. In this post, I will summarize the topic modeling process I conducted and describe the analytical approach I took. 

# Journal Summary

## 🧐 Research Question & Research Gap

Research Question: **Can combining LLM's context understanding and generation capabilities with existing clustering-based topic modeling generate more consistent and diverse topics than existing techniques?**

In this paper, the authors identify a research gap by stating that “They aren’t without limitations though. BERTopic, for example, is a clustering-based approach which suffers from limitations such as word representation overload or generation of only one topic per text.” To address this limitation, the authors propose a novel method called **QualIT**. It is the *Qualitative Insights Tool* to extend the capabilities of existing topic models. In this paper, the purpose is to generate more nuanced and interpretable topic representations from free-text data. Furthermore, it provide a powerful and scalable approach to automatically identify themes and patterns in large volumes of unstructured text data.

## 🗝️ Method

New method **LLM Enhanced Topic Modeling** consists of multiple steps to generate the main topics, which are then used towards determining sub-topics from documents. There are the three key steps in this approach.

### Step 1) Extract Key-phrases representing the individual document

For the step 1, the LLM prompt can extract multiple key phrases from the document, depending on its content. These key phrases are crucial for realizing the more complex elements of the core topic. 

### Step 2) Hallucination Check

For the next step, the paper progresses the *hallucination check*. It is to ensure the reliability of extracted key phrases, a **Choherence Score**, and it is calculated for each phrase.

This score measures the conformity and applicability of the following subjects by evaluating how effectively the key phrase corresponds with the actual text. In this term, key phrases with the *lowest coherence scores* called **hallucination**.

### Step 3) Clustering

For the final step, **partitional clustering algorithm (K-Means)** used to group the key-phrases identified in the previous step. The purpose of this step is to group documents into multiple clusters, each representing a collection of documents with similar semantic content. In this paper, two layers implied to find the main topics & sub-topics.
