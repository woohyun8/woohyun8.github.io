# Reading Summary

**[Deng et al. (2021)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0048969720355728?via%3Dihub)** served as the background knowledge for my project, offering key insights into how trade flows can be modeled and interpreted through network analysis.

## 🔍 Research Question & Research Gap

Although this paper does not explicitly state a research question in a single sentence, I believe I can summarize the research question as follows:

**"When viewed through social network analysis (SNA), what structural characteristics does virtual trade between major countries around the world have, not only in terms of the overall industry but also in terms of the primary, secondary, and tertiary industries?”**

Deng et al. (2021) analyzed the structure of virtual water trade networks among 19 major countries using social network analysis (SNA) to explore how global trade affects water resources.

The key point of this paper is that it did not simply look at total trade, but also divided it into **primary industry (agriculture), secondary industry (manufacturing), and tertiary industry (services)** to compare the characteristics across these industries.

The paper identifies two main **Research Gaps**:

1. Previous studies have mainly focused on agriculture, resulting in a lack of analysis of virtual water trade in the industrial and service sectors.
   
2. There has been no quantitative analysis of structural connectivity, such as centrality and density.

To fill these research gaps, the authors state:
> *“This study uses the multi-regional input–output table data and water use data obtained from the EORA database to conduct virtual water trade across the whole industry and three other major industries among major countries (the Group 20 countries, except the EU) from 2006 to 2015.”*

As a result, this paper comprehensively addresses the interactions between various industrial sectors and countries, and distinguishes itself from previous studies by analyzing virtual water flows at the global level.

## 📝 Method

This study uses a **multi-regional input–output (MRIO) model** to calculate virtual water trade among countries, and applies **social network analysis (SNA)** to investigate the structural characteristics of the trade network.

**1. Virtual Water Trade Calculation (Based on MRIO)**

First, calculate the **direct water use coefficient** for each country and industry, then use the following formula to derive the virtual water trade matrix:

$$
H = \hat{W} \cdot L \cdot Z
$$

- W: Direct water coefficient matrix  
- L : Leontief inverse matrix  
- Z : Final demand matrix

The matrix 𝐻 represents the **virtual water trade** between countries. From this, the virtual water trade matrix 𝑇 is derived by aggregating the flows by country and industry. The off-diagonal elements of 𝑇 indicate the bilateral virtual water exports and imports.

**2. Social Network Analysis (SNA)**

To analyze the structure of the virtual water trade network, the study use social network analysis (SNA). Key indicators include:

- **Density**:

$$
D = \frac{\sum_{r \ne s, r=1}^{m} \sum_{s=1}^{m} t^{rs}}{m(m - 1)}
$$

-> Measures the average strength of trade connections among countries.

- **Asymmetry**:

$$
S = \frac{\sum_{r \ne s, r=1}^{m} \sum_{s=1}^{m} \left| t^{rs} - t^{sr} \right|}{m(m - 1)}
$$

-> Represent the imbalance between virtual water exports and imports.

- **Out- Degree / In-Degree**:

$$
OD = \sum_{s \ne r, s=1}^{m} t^{rs} \quad , \quad ID = \sum_{r \ne s, r=1}^{m} t^{rs}
$$

-> Represent the total virtual water export & import volumes of a given country within the network.

The paper uses these indicators to quantitatively compare which countries play a core hub role in trade networks and how the connection structures differ by industry.

## 📉 In Degree / Out Degree

```python
density_maize = []

for i in range(1986, 2024):

    lm_i = lm[lm['Year'] == i]
    
    node_i = node[node['Year'] == i].drop_duplicates(subset='country')

    g_i = nx.from_pandas_edgelist(lm_i,
                                   source='O_name',
                                   target='D_name',
                                   edge_attr='Final Value',
                                   create_using=nx.DiGraph())

    node_attr = node_i.set_index('country').to_dict('index')
    nx.set_node_attributes(g_i, node_attr)

    out_degree_dict = dict(g_i.out_degree(weight='Final Value'))
    in_degree_dict = dict(g_i.in_degree(weight='Final Value'))
    
    nx.set_node_attributes(g_i, out_degree_dict, 'i_degree')
    nx.set_node_attributes(g_i, in_degree_dict, 'o_degree')
    
    for country in set(in_degree_dict.keys()).union(out_degree_dict.keys()):
        density_maize.append({
            'Year': i,
            'Country': country,
            'In Degree': in_degree_dict.get(country, 0),
            'Out Degree': out_degree_dict.get(country, 0)
        })
        
    for country in set(in_degree_dict.keys()).union(out_degree_dict.keys()):
        if country in gm.nodes:
            gm.nodes[country]['i_degree'] = in_degree_dict.get(country, 0)
            gm.nodes[country]['o_degree'] = out_degree_dict.get(country, 0)

df_maize_degree = pd.DataFrame(density_maize)
df_maize_degree
```
